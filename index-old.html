I am a fifth-year PhD candidate in the Department of Statistics at the University of Chicago, advised by Professor Aaron Schein. I expect to graduate in June 2027 and am on the postdoc market for Fall 2027.

My research involves development of scalable Bayesian methods for large, structured datasets, such as, temporal point processes for event data from different news sources and customer transactions data. Inference on such models involves use of amortized variational inference and modern machine learning tools. I have also worked with Professor Nikolaos Ignatiadis on empirical Bayes methods for denoising. I care about models that are both interpretable and fast enough to fit to large-scale real data, and my applications range from international relations event streams to customer purchase histories.

Before Chicago, I spent two years as a risk-modeling specialist at UBS and completed an M.Stat. at the Indian Statistical Institute, Kolkata.

Research

Bayesian modeling of spatiotemporal event data with low-rank Neyman–Scott tensor processes Best Poster, ISBA 2026

with Aaron Schein

Consider records of the form "news source s reports that actor i took action a toward actor j at time t." I develop a gradient-based, scalable temporal point process model for such large-scale event data that infers latent events interpretable as "major stories," which generate related events over long periods. The model combines neural point processes, probabilistic non-negative tensor decomposition, and scalable approximate Bayesian inference. Although motivated by international relations data, it applies broadly to marked temporal event data.

Amortized variational inference for customer-base analysis

with Aaron Schein

"Buy-'til-you-die" models are the workhorse of customer lifetime value estimation, but they typically assume Poisson purchase timing and rely on inference that does not scale to modern customer bases. This work develops scalable amortized variational inference for non-Poisson buy-'til-you-die models, making richer timing assumptions practical at scale.

Stein's unbiased risk estimate and score matching

with Nikolaos Ignatiadis, Frederic Koehler, and Amber Lee

We connect Stein's unbiased risk estimate (SURE) with Hyvärinen's score matching, two classical ideas that turn out to be closely related, and use the connection to develop and analyze empirical Bayes estimators for denoising problems.

Retrospective inference from recalled time-to-event data

with Debasis Sengupta

When people are asked to recall when an event happened, their answers are both imprecise and sometimes wrong. We propose a model that accounts for both inaccuracy and imprecision in recall while addressing confounding between correctly and incorrectly recalled observations, and apply it to survey data on the age at menarche.
